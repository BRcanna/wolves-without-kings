# Fiction, Cultural Specificity and Safety Boundary

The game can depict organized crime, drugs, extortion, corruption, smuggling and violence in detail as fiction while keeping the mechanics focused on simulation and consequence rather than reproducing actionable real-world criminal procedure.

## Cultural rule

Bulgaria is a setting, not a synonym for organized crime. Civilian life, work, family, class, regional identity, history, architecture, music, politics, migration, ordinary business and generational change must be present strongly enough that the criminal world feels like one layer of a society rather than the entire society.

## Research rule

Use real geography, history and cultural references to improve plausibility, then fictionalize organizations, routes, exact criminal procedures, operational vulnerabilities and sensitive methods. Real-world legal and historical review should be separated from gameplay abstraction.

## Mechanics rule

For drugs, laundering, smuggling, evasion and weapons:
- model roles, risk, scarcity, social consequence, evidence, relationships and economics;
- avoid step-by-step operational recipes;
- prefer fictionalized constraints and abstracted processes;
- never make the fun depend on teaching the player a transferable real-world criminal procedure.

This boundary is compatible with a mature, grounded crime game. It mainly changes *how* specificity is expressed: through people, history, consequence and systems rather than procedural instruction.
