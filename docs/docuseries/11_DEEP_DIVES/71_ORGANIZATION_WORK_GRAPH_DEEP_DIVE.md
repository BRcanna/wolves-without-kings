# Deep Dive — Criminal Organization Work Graph

The organization should feel like people doing work, not a menu generating passive income.

Hidden_Canopy-Crew provides the right conceptual donor: roles, claims, leases, dependencies, capability checks, review and recovery.

## Operation decomposition

A high-level operation becomes a graph.

Example fictional vehicle-export operation:

```text
identify suitable asset(s)
        |
        v
acquire vehicle ----> secure temporary storage
        |                     |
        +----------+----------+
                   v
            documentation task
                   |
                   v
         schedule transport window
                   |
          +--------+--------+
          v                 v
      driver task        contact task
          |                 |
          +--------+--------+
                   v
              settlement
```

No step describes real theft/evasion procedure; it describes organization dependencies.

## Member state

Each member has:

- roles;
- capabilities;
- current availability;
- region/location;
- relationship with leadership;
- reliability history;
- risk tolerance;
- current injuries/legal problems;
- personal priorities;
- work claims.

A nominal “driver” can refuse or fail because their child is sick, they were arrested, they are angry at the player, or the route is outside their competence.

## Claims and leases

When work is assigned, the member receives a claim with a time window. If they disappear, disconnect (online), get arrested or miss the deadline, the claim expires.

The operation then enters recovery instead of silently succeeding or permanently deadlocking.

## Review

After work completes, outcomes need verification:

- goods arrived;
- money reconciled;
- damage occurred;
- witnesses created;
- route compromised;
- member lied;
- member performed exceptionally.

Review can be automatic for objective world facts plus social interpretation by leadership.

## Delegation progression

Early career: player performs most work personally.

Middle career: player performs high-risk/interesting work and delegates routine tasks.

Late career: player chooses between hands-on involvement and leadership. The organization should be capable of operating without turning the game into a spreadsheet because failed/important work generates playable situations.

## Work creates content

Delegated work can produce opportunities:

- member calls for help;
- police stop interrupts route;
- rival interference;
- vehicle breakdown;
- internal theft;
- unexpected profitable opportunity;
- relationship dispute.

This is a source of emergent missions without mission rails.

## Online

Organization work is ideal asynchronous MMO content. Members can log in and claim available tasks, while NPC subordinates handle lower-tier tasks. The game retains responsibility/ownership through leases and server authority.

## Internal politics

Work history feeds promotion. A member repeatedly trusted with money and people develops a different status than someone used only for violence.

Succession therefore has evidence: who has actually carried the organization?
