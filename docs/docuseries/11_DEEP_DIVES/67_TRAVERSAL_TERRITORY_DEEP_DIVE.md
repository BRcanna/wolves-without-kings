# Deep Dive — Traversal, Territory and Neighborhood Knowledge

District-B13 influence belongs in the **route topology and movement philosophy**, not copied set pieces.

## Six route layers

A dense district should be authored simultaneously as:

1. street network;
2. alley/service network;
3. interior public network;
4. interior private/semi-private network;
5. roof/balcony/fire-escape network;
6. transit/vehicle network.

A strong block has cross-links between them.

## Route edge properties

Each traversal edge can carry:

```text
movement type
physical difficulty
required reach/strength/technique
noise
visibility
carried-weight tolerance
injury sensitivity
access ownership
schedule availability
familiarity value
pursuer capability
```

A fire escape may be physically easy but visible. An apartment shortcut may require social access rather than athletics. A rooftop jump may be fast but unsafe with a leg injury.

## Familiarity advantage

The neighborhood does not physically change because the player has a high familiarity stat. Instead familiarity changes what the character recognizes and how confidently they use it.

Newcomer:
- obvious street exits;
- public stairwells;
- visible ladders.

Long-term resident:
- knows which courtyard gate sticks;
- knows a shop rear door is usually open before closing;
- recognizes a connected roofline;
- knows the tram timing;
- knows which tenant may help;
- knows which basement route is a dead end after rain.

## Territory

Territory is a bundle of access and influence:

- businesses paying protection;
- residents sympathetic/hostile;
- faction members present;
- safe properties;
- storage;
- lookout coverage;
- police attention;
- known traversal;
- nightlife control;
- landlord/property relationships.

No single “ownership percentage” should determine all of those.

## Pursuer asymmetry

Police, gang members and civilians do not all traverse the same graph equally.

A local teenage runner may be better on rooftops than an armed enforcer. A specialized police unit may handle some routes well. A heavy older boss may know the route but choose a car because their body condition makes the climb expensive.

## Vehicles are part of traversal

Vehicle transitions should be fluid. The player can pre-stage a motorcycle, switch cars, use public transit, move through a parking structure, cut through a pedestrian area and continue on foot.

The game needs explicit handoff points but should avoid obvious “vehicle section starts here” level design.

## Interior density

Freedom depends on enough usable interiors. Not every door needs a fully simulated apartment, but dense areas require a meaningful percentage of traversable/semi-traversable interiors so chases cannot be solved only on streets.

Use reusable interior families with stable identity, resident ownership and schedule/access conditions.

## World-aging effect

Routes change over years. Construction closes an alley. Renovation locks a roof access. A friendly shop becomes a chain store. A building is demolished. Old familiarity becomes partially stale.

That is an opportunity to make time progression physically legible.
