# Deep Dive — Bulgaria, Era and World Identity

The game’s setting should matter structurally, not only through flags, names and license plates.

This document is a design direction, not a substitute for Bulgarian historical/cultural research and review.

## Geographic identity

### Sofia

Primary dense hub:
- government/institutional power;
- business/finance;
- nightlife;
- large housing estates;
- wealthy residential pockets;
- industrial/logistics zones;
- university/youth culture;
- major transport links.

Sofia should contain enough socioeconomic contrast that traveling several kilometers changes the rules of social presentation.

### Black Sea

Use a compressed coastal network inspired by the roles of Varna, Burgas and resort areas without needing a one-to-one city recreation.

Systemic identity:
- ports/logistics;
- tourism seasonality;
- clubs/hospitality;
- construction/property;
- foreign visitors and international contacts;
- seasonal labor;
- luxury consumption;
- maritime access.

### Rural/mountain areas

Systemic identity:
- lower anonymity in small communities;
- difficult/beautiful roads;
- hidden property/storage;
- family history;
- older infrastructure;
- different policing/social visibility;
- strong value of regional familiarity.

## Social layers in the city

The earlier three-layer model remains useful but should overlap rather than map to separate zones.

**Polished layer**: hotels, offices, banks, government, luxury retail, formal restaurants, new property.

**Criminal-commercial layer**: garages, clubs, transport, security, construction, depots, markets, football networks, warehouses.

**Neglected/vertical layer**: aging estates, unfinished construction, rail edges, industrial ruins, dense courtyards, roofs, service access.

A single district can contain all three within walking distance.

## Era arc

If the campaign spans roughly the late 1990s into the 2010s, each era should change systems.

Examples of change categories:
- mobile phone availability and behavior;
- internet use;
- camera density;
- vehicles and imports;
- nightlife aesthetics;
- financial/business formality;
- police data sharing;
- construction boom/decline;
- international travel/contact expectations;
- music/fashion;
- public tolerance for visible criminal display.

Exact historical implementation requires dedicated research.

## Ordinary life

A crime game becomes more credible when most people are not criminals. Schools, commuting, work, shopping, family gatherings, sport, music, repair, weather, tourism and ordinary business should populate the simulation.

Criminal mechanics then feel transgressive because there is a normal world to disrupt.

## Avoiding stereotype collapse

Do not make every government worker corrupt, every housing estate criminal, every rural person backward, or every wealthy character mafia-adjacent. The simulation needs enough ordinary counterexamples that corruption/crime remains character-specific and institutional rather than national caricature.

## Language

Language can be a gameplay resource through formality, slang, generation and cross-border contacts, but the production should use native review for Bulgarian dialogue and naming. Machine-translated flavor text would undermine the entire grounding goal.
