# Deep Dive — Rumor and Information Network

A criminal world is largely an information world. Who knows what, who believes whom, and how quickly information travels should matter almost as much as combat skill.

## Four knowledge classes

1. **Observed** — the actor directly perceived something.
2. **Reported** — another actor/document communicated a claim.
3. **Inferred** — the actor concluded something from multiple observations/claims.
4. **Institutional** — a police/business/organization record contains a claim.

None of these automatically equals authoritative truth.

## Claim lineage

A story should have lineage.

```text
Event E42: rival shot outside club
  witness Ana observes partial scene
    -> Claim C1: “dark BMW left immediately”
      -> tells cousin -> C2: “Gerron’s BMW left”
        -> cousin tells bartender -> C3: “Gerron’s people did it”
```

The world truth may say someone else committed the shooting. The rumor can still change behavior.

## Distortion

Distortion is not random word salad. It should come from:

- missing detail;
- speaker bias;
- listener prior belief;
- social incentive;
- compression (“the story gets shorter”);
- deliberate deception;
- confusion of similar people/vehicles;
- generational retelling.

## Domain trust

One person can be reliable about cars and unreliable about politics. Trust therefore needs domains.

An experienced mechanic’s claim about a vehicle may carry weight even if people consider their gang gossip exaggerated.

## Information latency

A story in Sofia does not instantly update Varna. Phone calls, news, organization communications and travel determine propagation speed.

This makes regional reputation possible. The player can arrive somewhere before their latest reputation does—or discover an older version of themselves is still circulating.

## Deliberate reputation management

The player can influence the information network through actions:

- public generosity;
- visible brutality;
- denying a rumor;
- asking trusted people to vouch;
- allowing credit for something they did not do;
- keeping an event quiet;
- choosing witnesses carefully in a social sense.

The player cannot select “+10 reputation.” They act on the network.

## Institutional information

Police, businesses and organizations may have records that spread differently from street rumor. A detective report can be accurate but confidential. A newspaper can be public but simplified. A private organization ledger can be detailed but vulnerable to theft.

## Oral history and legacy

OWMH’s oral tradition concept is useful for long campaigns. Major events can become stories that survive after witnesses die. A successor may hear a distorted story about the founder they never personally experienced.

That gives dynasty mode cultural memory, not just inherited property.
