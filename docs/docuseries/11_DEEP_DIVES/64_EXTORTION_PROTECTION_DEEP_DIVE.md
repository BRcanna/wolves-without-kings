# Deep Dive — Extortion and Protection Economy

Protection is one of the game’s best candidate systems because it joins money, territory, relationships, violence, reputation, scheduling and police risk without requiring a scripted heist every ten minutes.

## Business state

Every protected business needs more than `pays_player=true`.

```text
Business
  sector
  owner / manager
  employees
  normal revenue band
  cashflow volatility
  local customers
  suppliers
  opening schedule
  property owner
  current protection claims
  recent incidents
  owner fear/trust/resentment
  ability to pay
  willingness to cooperate
  police/rival relationships
```

## Protection contract

A protection relationship can be explicit or informal.

```text
payment expectation
collection cadence
services expected
territorial claim
collector assigned
missed-payment policy
rival-interference policy
relationship history
```

The services side is important. The player can actually protect a business from theft, harassment, rival collections or neighborhood problems. A business that receives real value can develop trust even if the relationship began coercively.

## Collection is work

Collections are organization work items. A boss can collect personally early in their career and later delegate.

The assigned collector matters. A disciplined collector and a violent idiot can produce the same cash today and opposite long-term outcomes.

## Owner strategies

Owners are agents, not ATMs. They can:

- pay willingly;
- pay from fear;
- negotiate;
- delay;
- lie about revenue;
- ask for reduced terms;
- request real protection;
- secretly report;
- switch to a rival;
- sell the business;
- close;
- leave the region;
- become an informant;
- join the organization economically;
- decide the arrangement is useful.

## Over-extraction

The game needs business viability. If the organization extracts too much, the business becomes less able to pay. A closed shop produces zero protection revenue and changes the neighborhood.

This creates a reason for sophisticated players to care about the local economy without introducing a morality slider.

## Violence

Violence is an available instrument, not the default progression button. It can create immediate compliance while increasing resentment, witness evidence, family retaliation, police attention and business disruption.

The important design challenge is avoiding a dominant “always beat them once” strategy. That requires consequences to propagate through social and economic systems.

## Territory

Territory is not colored map paint. Protection territory is a network of actual relationships and practical ability to respond.

A crew can claim a block but have only three businesses actually paying. Another organization may have a personal relationship with one owner inside the same area. Police pressure may make collections difficult at certain times. A respected older owner may be effectively neutral.

## Information value

Protected businesses can become sensors in the social network. Owners see cars, strangers, police activity, gossip and changes in nearby property. A well-treated network may warn the player voluntarily. A terrified network may provide information only when directly asked—and may tell rivals the same thing.

## Six-month trace

Month 1: player approaches six businesses. Three pay from fear, one negotiates, one refuses, one already pays a rival.

Month 2: the player actually resolves a theft problem for the negotiating owner. Trust rises. The rival pressures the contested owner.

Month 3: an overaggressive collector injures an employee at one paying business. Revenue falls and resentment rises.

Month 4: police ask owners about recent collections. The trusted owner minimizes what they know; the injured business owner provides a useful description.

Month 5: rival conflict raises neighborhood risk. Two owners ask for more visible protection.

Month 6: player can stabilize, escalate, renegotiate, buy into one business, replace the collector or abandon the area.

That is a living racket rather than a weekly cash icon.
