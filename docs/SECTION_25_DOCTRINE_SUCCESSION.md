# Section 25 — Organization doctrine, internal politics, and succession

This episode composes docuseries Volume 16 into an executable, persistence-tested bounded slice over organization governance.

## Built

- doctrine rules distinguish permitted, forbidden, tolerated, civilian, family, foreign, and police-policy bands;
- members evaluate doctrine through their own values and scoped knowledge, so compliance, pause, and deviation remain choices with consequences rather than mind control;
- leadership succession scores seniority, relationship history, capability, coalition support, discipline, and cause of vacancy;
- the same organization can resolve into a consolidated successor, fragmented factions, or a caretaker state depending on relationship graphs;
- doctrine updates require the current leader or council authority and age into a generational-tension band over time;
- public projection exposes qualitative doctrine, status, and coalition counts while omitting exact values, relationship scores, support, discipline history, candidate scores, and event internals;
- hash-linked snapshots preserve the history and reject stale or tampered writes.

## Acceptance evidence

`npm test` includes `doctrine.test.mjs`, which proves three succession outcomes, value-mediated doctrine decisions, authority/time behavior, public redaction, and snapshot integrity.

## Boundary

This is a fictional organization-governance model. It does not encode real-world criminal logistics, recruitment, coercion, evasion, or operational procedure.
